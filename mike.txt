Tariffs are taxes on imported goods. Governments use them to protect domestic industries or raise revenue. French wine producers suffered when bourbon tariffs reduced American demand for European products. The IMF estimates that trade wars reduce global GDP. Supply chains become inefficient. Innovation slows when competition decreases.

Evidence suggests tariffs create winners and losers. Protected industries benefit temporarily. But consumers and downstream industries pay the price. The ILO notes that trade protection saves some jobs but destroys others. History shows that extreme protectionism deepened the Great Depression. Modern economies are interconnected. Balanced trade policy must weigh short-term protection against long-term growth. Free trade with targeted exceptions may offer the best path.

Pakistan's flour controls created smuggling and hoarding. Short-term political gain comes with long-term economic pain. Better policies include targeted subsidies for the poor. These help vulnerable groups without distorting markets. Price controls treat symptoms, not causes. Addressing supply issues works better than capping prices.

ESSAY 5: INFLATION
================================================================================

Chile experienced devastating inflation during Salvador Allende's presidency from 1970 to 1973. Prices rose over 600 percent in 1973 alone. This hyperinflation destroyed savings and created chaos. Inflation means a general increase in prices over time. It reduces the purchasing power of money. Two key causes drive inflation: excessive money supply growth and supply-side shocks that reduce available goods.

Demand-pull inflation occurs when too much money chases too few goods. Chile's government printed money to fund spending programs. The central bank expanded the money supply by 100 percent annually. Workers demanded higher wages to keep up with rising prices. This created a wage-price spiral. The IMF notes that money supply growth consistently predicts inflation. Ukraine faced similar problems in 2014-2015. Political instability and currency printing drove inflation to 43 percent in 2015. People rushed to buy goods before prices rose further. This panic buying accelerated inflation. Savings became worthless overnight.

Cost-push inflation stems from supply disruptions. Ukraine's crisis included supply shocks from war in the eastern regions. Energy costs surged. Food production fell. The World Bank reported that Ukraine's GDP contracted by 10 percent in 2015. When production costs rise, businesses increase prices. The 1970s oil crisis demonstrated this globally. OPEC reduced oil supply. Energy prices quadrupled. This pushed up costs for all goods. Transportation, manufacturing, and heating became more expensive. Central banks struggled to control inflation without causing recession.

Evidence shows that controlling inflation requires careful policy. The BIS emphasizes that central banks must maintain credibility. Chile eventually stabilized by reducing money growth and controlling spending. Ukraine received IMF support to anchor inflation expectations. Moderate inflation around 2 percent is healthy. It encourages spending and investment. But high inflation destroys economic stability. Price signals become meaningless. Planning becomes impossible. Sound monetary policy prevents inflation from spiraling out of control.

ESSAY 6: GROSS DOMESTIC PRODUCT (GDP)
================================================================================

Norway has the world's largest sovereign wealth fund worth over $1.4 trillion from oil revenues. Yet Norwegians debate whether GDP growth truly reflects their wellbeing. GDP measures the total value of goods and services produced in an economy. It serves as the primary indicator of economic health. Two perspectives clash: GDP accurately measures prosperity versus GDP ignores crucial aspects of human welfare.

Supporters argue GDP provides essential economic information. Higher GDP means more jobs and income. Norway's oil GDP funded extensive social programs. The OECD uses GDP to compare national economic performance. Countries with higher GDP per capita generally have better infrastructure. Malta's gaming industry contributes 12 percent to its GDP. This sector created thousands of jobs. GDP growth indicates business expansion. It helps governments plan budgets. The World Bank relies on GDP data to allocate development aid. Investors use GDP trends to make decisions. Without GDP, measuring economic progress would be difficult.

Critics point out GDP's serious limitations. It counts harmful activities as positive. Oil extraction increases GDP but damages the environment. GDP ignores unpaid work like childcare and volunteering. Norway's happiness surveys show wealth doesn't guarantee life satisfaction. The UNEP notes that GDP treats natural resource depletion as income, not loss. A country can boost GDP by cutting all its forests. This is clearly not sustainable prosperity. GDP also misses inequality. Malta's gaming wealth concentrates in few hands. Average GDP says nothing about poverty levels. Healthcare spending increases GDP, but sickness is not progress.

Evidence confirms we need broader measures of success. The UNDP created the Human Development Index to supplement GDP. It includes health, education, and living standards. Norway tops this index because it invests oil wealth wisely. GDP remains useful but incomplete. Policymakers should track environmental health, happiness, and equality alongside economic output. Sustainable development requires looking beyond simple production numbers. GDP tells us about quantity. We also need measures of quality.
